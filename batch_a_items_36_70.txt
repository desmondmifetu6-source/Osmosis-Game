58. [adiabatic]
1. A term used to describe any thermodynamic process that occurs with neither a gain nor loss of energy between the system and its surroundings; or any thermodynamic process that occurs without heat entering or leaving a system. Since no heat enters nor leaves the system, heat (Q) is zero, hence the first law of thermodynamic becomes: ΔE in = -W, where ΔE in is the internal energy of the system and W is the work done. This process generally involves a fall or rise in temperature of the system. The term can be applied in several contexts as follows: (i) Electronically adiabatic is a reaction in which there is no change of electronic state or multiplicity. (ii) Vibrational adiabaticity is a reaction in which there is no change of vibrational state during the course of reaction. The term also applies to a process in which excess vibrational energy in the reactants appears as vibrational energy in the products or in which ground-state vibration in the reactants leads to ground state vibration in the products. (iii) Rotational adiabatic is a reaction in which excess rotational energy in the reactants appears as rotational energy in the products or in which ground- state rotation in the reactants leads to ground-state rotation in the products. 2. In the Rice-Ramsperger-Kassel-Marcus (RRKM) theory of unimolecular reactions, a degree of freedom whose quantum number is more or less preserved during energisation.
