The law that the force exerted between two electric or magnetic charges is directly proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. [270] curie's law
A law which states that the susceptibility (x) of a paramagnetic substance is inversely proportional to the thermodynamic temperature ( T ). This can be expressed as M = C . B / T , where M is the resulting magnetisation, B is the magnetic field, measured in teslas, T is absolute temperature, measured in kelvins and C is a material-specific Curie constant. The law is named after Pierre Curie (1859-1906), a French physicist, a pioneer in crystallography, magnetism, piezoelectricity and radioactivity.

[277] cyanogen
A colourless, highly poisonous gas, with the chemical formula C2 N2 , relative density 0.95, melting point^-28 °C (245 K) and boiling point^-21 °C (252 K). It is made by the action of acids on cyanides. It is very toxic, as it readily undergoes reduction to cyanide and can poison the cytochrome c oxidase complex, thus interrupting the mitochondrial electron transfer chain. It is used as a rocket propellant, an insecticide and a chemical weapon.
