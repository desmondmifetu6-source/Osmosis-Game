=== 1. [28] earnshaw's theorem ===
RAW: 
DEF: It states that a collection of point charges cannot be maintained in a stable static equilibrium configuration solely by the electrostatic interaction of the charges. This theorem was first proved by Samuel Earnshaw in 1842 and it is a fundamental result concerning bodies that interact according to inverse square laws, such as Coulomb's law of electrostatics and Newton's law of gravitation. It applies to the classical law forces (electric and gravitational) and also to the magnetic forces of permanent magnets and paramagnetic materials but not diamagnetic materials.

=== 4. [102] economy ===
RAW: 
DEF: A mathematical model of an economic system in which, commonly, there are m producers, each with a production set P j and n consumers, each with a consumption set C i and an associated ordering ≤ i . These sets lie on an Euclidean space, whose dimension corresponds to the number of goods in the economy. There is a total resource level w . The producers each produce a vector y i in P j and the consumers have a demand x i in C i . These m + n elements define a state of the economy. The excess demand is ∑ x i - ∑ y j - w and a market equilibrium occurs if excess demand is zero. Individual consumers will try to maximize their preference satisfaction; this leads to the study of equilibria that occur as a market equilibrium together with a pricing of resources that every producer maximizes profit and every consumer optimizes satisfaction.

=== 5. [126] eddington limit ===
RAW: 
DEF: A theoretical limit for the maximum value of the brightness of a star of a given E mass, named after British astronomer and physicist, Arthur Stanley Eddington and given as L = 4 π GMm p c σ T , where G is the gravitational pressure, M is the mass of the luminous object, m P is the mass of a proton, c is the speed of light and σ T is the effective area of an electron when it is illuminated by radiation. This limit exists because the radiation pressure caused by the nuclear fission reactions powering the star has to counter, but not exceed the gravitational force that would cause gravitational collapse of the star. A star emitting radiation at greater than the Eddington limit would break up. In a star it is defined as the point where the gravitational force inwards equals the continuum radiation force outwards, assuming hydrostatic equilibrium and spherical symmetry. When exceeding the Eddington luminosity, a star would initiate a very intense continuum driven stellar wind from its outer layers. Since most massive stars have luminosities far below the Eddington limit, however, their winds are mostly driven by the less intense line absorption.

=== 6. [161] effective nuclear charge ===
RAW: 
DEF: The apparent nuclear or net positive charge which an outermost electron actually experiences through attraction in a multi-electron atom as a result of the shielding effect by other electrons. Its symbol is Z eff . Since the shielding effect of negatively charged electrons prevents higher orbital electrons from experiencing the full nuclear charge by the repelling effect of inner- layered electrons, the term 'effective' is used to qualify the nuclear charge. The strength of the nuclear charge can be determined by looking at the oxidation number of the atom. Where there is only one electron in an atom, that electron experiences the full charge of the positive nucleus, in which case, the effective nuclear charge can be calculated from Coulomb's law. However, in a multi-electron atom the outer electrons are simultaneously attracted to the positive nucleus E and repelled by the negatively charged electrons. The effective nuclear charge on such an electron is given by the expression: Z eff = Z - S, where, Z is the number of protons in the nucleus (atomic number) and S is the average number of electrons between the nucleus and the electron in question (the number of nonvalence electrons).

=== 17. [213] einstein-smoluchowski equation ===
RAW: 
DEF: An unexpected connection revealed independently by Albert Einstein in 1905 and Marian Smoluchowski in 1906 in their papers on Brownian motion, which is a relationship between the diffusion coefficient D and the distance λ that a particle can jump in a time τ . The equation is given as D = λ 2 /2 τ and it gives a connection between the microscopic details of particle diffusion and the macroscopic quantities associated with the diffusion. This equation is an early example of a fluctuation-dissipation relation and it is frequently used in the electro diffusion phenomena.

=== 20. [230] elastic modules ===
RAW: 
DEF: The ratio of the stress applied to a body to the strain produced or the slope of its stress-strain curve in the elastic deformation region. It is the mathematical description of an object's tendency to be deformed elastically, when a force is applied to it. It may be expressed as λ = stress strain , where λ (lambda) is the elastic modulus, stress the force causing the deformation divided by the area to which the force is applied and strain, the ratio of the change caused by the stress to the original state of the object. If stress is measured in pascals, since strain is a unitless ratio, then the units of λ are also pascals. Due to the fact that the denominator of the above equation becomes unity if length is doubled, the elastic modulus becomes the stress needed to cause a sample of the material to double in length. Since this endpoint is not realistic because most materials will fail before reaching it, it is practical, due to the fact that small fractions of the defining load will operate in the same ratio.

=== 24. [253] electric displacement ===
RAW: 
DEF: The charge per unit area that would be displaced across a layer of a conductor placed across an electric field. Its symbol is D and it stands for displacement. The electric displacement field D is, therefore, defined as D ≡ ε 0 E + P , where ε 0 is the permittivity in free space, E is the actual average value of the electric field and P is the (macroscopic) density of the permanent and induced electric dipole moments in the material, known as the polarisation density. === 25. [255] electric field ===
RAW:

=== 35. [352] electron capture ===
RAW: 
DEF: 1. A radioactive transformation process for isotopes that have too many protons in the nucleus and insufficient energy to emit a positron. In this case, one of the orbital electrons, usually from the K or L shell is captured by a proton in nucleus, thus forming a neutron and a neutrino (neutral elementary particle). Since the proton is changed to a neutron the atomic number decreases by 1, the nuclide is transformed into a new element. But the atomic mass number remains unchanged. Examples of nuclides that undergo electron capture are 41 Ca, 51 Cr, 56 Ni, etc. 2. The formation of a negative ion by an atom or molecule when it acquires an extra free electron or a radioactive transformation in which a nucleus acquires an electron from an inner orbit of the atom thereby transforming initially into a nucleus with the same mass number but an atomic number one less than that of the original nucleus. It is accompanied by emission of an x-ray photon or Auger electron as the vacancy in the inner orbit is filled by an outer electron. If the energy difference between the parent atom and the daughter atom is less than 1.022 MeV, positron emission is forbidden and electron capture is the sole decay mode. For example, Rubidium^-83 will decay to Krypton^-83 only by electron capture (the energy difference is about 0.9 MeV).
