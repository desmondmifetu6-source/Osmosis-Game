=== CANDIDATE 11 / 40 | Index: 85 | Word: gamma radiation ===
Definition:
An electromagnetic radiation emitted by excited atomic nucleus when it moves to a lower excitation state. Gamma radiation lies above X-Rays in the electromagnetic spectrum. It is emitted by the hottest part of the universe and also emitted by the radioactive decay of certain materials like cobalt^-60, zinc^-65, caesium^-137, and radium^-226. Cadmium^-109 is also a common source of gamma radiation. Gamma rays can be produced in laboratories by exciting nuclei of a radioactive element by the use of cyclotron and synchrotron. Gamma radiation is one of the three types of radiation resulting from natural radioactivity. It is the most energetic form of electromagnetic radiation, with the shortest wavelength (high frequency). Gamma radiation ranges in energy from about 10 -15 to 10 -10 joule (10 keV to 10 MeV) corresponding to a wavelength range of about 10 -10 to 10 -14 metre. It is so highly penetrating that 25mm of lead only halves its strength. The speed of gamma rays is equal to the speed of light since it is a type of electromagnetic wave and all electromagnetic waves travel with the speed of light. Gamma rays produced in space are absorbed by the earth's atmosphere. Gamma radiation is used for cancer treatment and for diagnostic purposes. They are also used for killing the harmful bacteria in food (a process called food irradiation) so that it can be preserved for longer duration. It is also used for inspection of castings and welds, etc.

=== CANDIDATE 24 / 40 | Index: 170 | Word: gauss' law ===
Definition:
A law formulated by Carl Friedrich Gauss in 1835 relating the distribution of electric charge to the resulting electric field. It states that the total electric flux normal to a closed surface in an electric field is proportional to the algebraic sum of the electric charges within the surface. A similar law applies to the surfaces, drawn in a magnetic field and the law can be generalised for any vector field through a closed surface. It is one of four of Maxwell's equations which form the basis of classical electrodynamics; the other three being Gauss's law for magnetism, Faraday's law of induction and Ampère's law with Maxwell's correction. Gauss's law can be used to derive Coulomb's law and vice versa.
